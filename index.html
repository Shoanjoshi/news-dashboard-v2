T5 K-Shaped Economy and Income Inequality OVERVIEW:
Recent U.S. economic growth has been driven primarily by high-income households, creating a "K-shaped" divergence where affluent consumers spend and earn significantly more than lower-income groups. This unequal distribution reflects broader structural economic challenges including wealth concentration, differential inflation impacts, and rising food insecurity among vulnerable populations.

KEY EXAMPLES:
- High-income households (earning >$125,000/year) drove retail spending growth since 2023, while lower-income households saw stagnant or declining real consumption
- During the March 2026 energy price surge, high-income households maintained real gasoline consumption while low-income households cut consumption despite higher nominal spending due to inflation
- Food insecurity surged among lower-educated and lower-income households, correlating with increased consumer pessimism in these groups
- Wealth gains concentrated among high-income households since 2023, while inflation hit lower-income households hardest
- K-shaped spending patterns observed both nationally and in the Second District region		 29
