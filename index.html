T2 ** K-Shaped Economy and Income Inequality **OVERVIEW:**
Recent U.S. economic data reveals a widening divergence in consumer spending and economic outcomes between high-income and low-income households since 2023. This "K-shaped" pattern—where higher earners experience stronger growth than lower earners—reflects differences in wealth accumulation, inflation exposure, and spending behavior across income groups.

**KEY EXAMPLES:**
- High-income households (earning over $125,000 annually) have driven retail spending growth since 2023, while lower-income households have lagged behind
- During the March 2026 energy price surge, high-income households maintained real gasoline consumption while low-income households reduced consumption despite higher nominal spending
- Wealth has increased most for high-income households while inflation has risen most for low-income households, explaining divergent spending patterns
- Earnings growth has been mixed, though the highest earners have experienced faster income growth than the lowest earners		 34
