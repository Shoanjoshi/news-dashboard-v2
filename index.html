T3 U.S.-China Trade Tensions and Supply Chain Shifts OVERVIEW:
Despite significant changes to U.S. trade policy toward China over the past year, overall trade deficits have remained largely unchanged, while China's global trade surplus actually increased. However, bilateral trade flows between the U.S., China, and Southeast Asia have shifted substantially, with broader geopolitical challenges—including Middle East conflicts and critical mineral dependencies—complicating U.S.-China trade relations.

KEY EXAMPLES:
- The U.S. twelve-month trade deficit ended 2025 at $1.2 trillion, nearly unchanged from 2024, while China's global trade surplus rose from $1 trillion to $1.2 trillion
- China's advanced technology industries (electric vehicles, lithium batteries, solar panels) have driven increases in its trade surplus through improved price and quality
- The U.S. faces dependency on China for rare-earth minerals needed to rebuild weapons stockpiles, particularly following conflicts in the Middle East
- Southeast Asian supply chains, critical for AI infrastructure and heavily reliant on Middle East energy, present vulnerabilities to global supply shocks
- The administration has had to scale back initially planned harsh trade moves on China while considering additional tariffs, such as a proposed 10% tariff over drug inflow		 33
