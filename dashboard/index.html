T0 ** K-Shaped Recovery and Income Inequality **OVERVIEW:**
Since 2023, the U.S. economy has experienced unequal growth, with high-income households driving retail spending gains while lower-income households lag behind. This divergence reflects broader structural economic challenges including wealth concentration, differential inflation impacts, and labor market dynamics.

**KEY EXAMPLES:**
- High-income households (earning >$125,000) have driven retail spending growth since 2023, while lower-income households have seen slower consumption increases
- Wealth increased most for high-income households while inflation rose most for low-income households, explaining divergent spending patterns
- In March 2026, high-income households maintained real gasoline consumption during energy price spikes, while low-income households reduced real consumption despite higher nominal spending
- Long-term structural changes affecting interest rates (R*) also influence labor share and wages, with implications for macroeconomic outcomes		 35
